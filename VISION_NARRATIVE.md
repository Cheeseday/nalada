# Nalada — Project Vision

## What this is
This project looks at one big question: do countries have to pollute more as they get richer? It's something almost everyone assumes is true - but I want to actually check it with data, not just take it for granted.

## The premise
For a long time, getting richer and emitting more CO2 went hand in hand. Economies grew, cities grew, factories ran, and emissions climbed right alongside them. It looked like a rule you couldn't escape: more wealth meant more pollution, full stop.

But lately, some countries seem to have pulled the two apart - their economies kept growing while their emissions actually went down. If that's real, it's a genuinely big deal. But it might not be. A country can look clean simply by moving its dirty industries somewhere else and buying the products back. The pollution doesn't go away - it just stops showing up in *their* numbers.

So the real question is: when a country says it went green, did it actually? Or did it just hand the problem to someone poorer?

## How the project works
The analysis moves in two directions at once, and that movement is the whole idea:

**It zooms in.** Starting with the whole world, then narrowing to European countries, then down to individual cities. Each step gives up some of the big picture to see what's actually happening underneath.

**It gets more honest.** It starts with the emissions countries report for themselves - what gets produced inside their borders. Then it shifts to a harder number: what people actually *consume*, including everything they import. The space between those two numbers is where you find out whether "green growth" is real or just good bookkeeping.

## The three chapters

**Chapter 1 - Context.** How do city growth, wealth, and emissions actually fit together around the world? Before asking who escaped the pattern, I want to understand the pattern itself - and question the easy assumption that packing people into cities is what causes the damage. The goal is to tell apart the things that just happen to move together from the thing that's really driving it.

**Chapter 2 - Decoupling.** Which countries grew their economies while cutting emissions - and which of them did it for real? This is the core of the project: a way to check a country's progress against what it actually consumes and trades, to see whether the carbon was truly cut or just shipped abroad.

**Chapter 3 - Cities.** For the countries that genuinely broke the link, what did they do differently on the ground? This chapter gets down to street level - how people get around (cars, transit, bikes) and how cities are built (green space, land, waste). The question is whether real change leaves a visible mark in how a city is actually put together.

## What it's really about
On the surface, it's about CO2. Underneath, it's about being honest with numbers - how the same facts can look good or bad depending on where you draw the line, and how easy it is to draw that line in the flattering place. The project isn't here to praise or blame any country. It's here to set up the measurements fairly and then follow them wherever they lead - from the whole planet down to a city streets.
