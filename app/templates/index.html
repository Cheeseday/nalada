The limits of this analysis

This story accuses others of giving flattering reviews - so it has its caveats. Before you trust one rating, there're four things to keep in mind.

Consumption figures are modelled, not measured. Consumption CO₂ is reconstructed with multi-regional input-output models that estimate the CO₂ contained in trade. Models inherit uncertainty from global trade tables and sector aggregation - they reflect the trend, not a precise level.

Small/trade-heavy economies. Ireland's profit-shifted GDP, Luxembourg's cross-border workforce and tiny Malta distort per capita comparisons, so I flagged or set aside them rather than ranked by objective criteria.

The scope is deliberately narrow. This is a Europe-only study - just 34 countries - so the sample is small and the patterns describe Europe, not the world at large.

The honesty weighting is a choice. Scoring the index 40% on honesty is a deliberate decision, not a law of nature - different weights would reshuffle the board (i suspect, not as significantly as you might imagine).
